# Music App — Design Brief & Ideation

## Core Philosophy
**Certainty over discovery anxiety.** The app doesn't make you hunt for something to play. Even random feels confident. The contract: *you already know yourself, we trust that.*

Convention isn't wrong — recently played and recommendations have their place — but they shouldn't be the default mode, and the user should be able to signal context without friction.

---

## The Loading Screen as Thesis Statement
Somewhere between:
- A video game loading cinematic (intentional, atmospheric)
- The Chrome offline dino (playful, immediate, interactive)
- Infinite generative design (never the same twice)

It should encapsulate what music is to the user — not a wait, but an entry point.

---

## Aesthetic Language

### Primary Reference: HK Cinema / Wong Kar-wai
- Film grain, green-tinted shadows, dark corridors, tunnel driving
- 90s cam texture — warmth through imperfection
- Grim but beautiful. Melancholic but certain.

### Secondary Reference: Son of the White Mare (Fehérlófia)
- Mythological density — every corner rewards looking
- Swirling, organic detail at multiple scales
- Cosmic and earthy at the same time

### Visual Grammar (from Pinterest references)
- **Luminous focal points inside darkness** — iridescent waves, fractal stars, glowing portals
- **Negative space as breath** — minimal temple/bonsai moments between dense sections
- **Retro pixel + cosmic hi-fi coexistence** — lo-fi and transcendent can share a screen
- **Illustration as UI** — genre, mood, era expressed visually, not just as text tags

---

## UX Principles

1. **Pre-play certainty** — the app earns trust before you hit play
2. **Loading = experience** — interactive, never blank, never just a spinner
3. **Context modes** — recently played / recommendations exist but aren't imposed
4. **Music is art → app is art** — aesthetic coherence is a feature, not polish
5. **Multi-format genre as visual language** — generated illustrations per genre if needed

## Key Design Challenge
How do you visually represent wildly different genres (hyperpop, folk, ambient, metal, classical) in a single visual system that feels coherent and like art — not a tag cloud or category grid?

**Answer direction:** Generated or curated illustrations per genre/mood, with a consistent dark-luminous grammar that makes everything feel like it belongs to the same world.
